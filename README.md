# CS250-Sprint-Review-and-Retrospective
## CS 250 Journal Reflection

### 1. How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by reviewing requirements, identifying the problems the program needs to solve, and determining what features will provide the best solution. User stories help me understand the application from the user's perspective and break requirements into smaller, manageable tasks. In my SNHU Travel project, I created a user story for the Top Five Destination List and used acceptance criteria to identify what the feature needed to include, such as five destinations, descriptions, pictures, and links to travel packages. This helped me understand how to turn user expectations into specific requirements that I could implement in a program. I learned that clear user stories and acceptance criteria help reduce confusion, guide development, and make it easier to determine whether a feature meets the user's needs.

### 2. How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by reviewing the requirements, planning the work, making changes in manageable steps, and checking that the results meet the expected goals. My experience in CS 250 taught me that software development requires flexibility because requirements can change throughout a project. While working on the SNHU Travel project, I used Java and Eclipse to customize the destination list and slideshow based on the requested features and updated instructions. In my future development work, I hope to incorporate Agile practices such as sprint planning, daily progress updates, sprint reviews, and retrospectives. These practices can help me organize tasks, identify problems early, receive feedback, and continuously improve my work. I also want to use user stories and acceptance criteria to keep development focused on the most important requirements.

### 3. What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for assigned tasks, respecting other people's ideas, and working toward a shared goal. My experience learning the Scrum Master, Product Owner, and Developer roles helped me understand how different responsibilities contribute to a successful project. I learned that team members need to share progress, discuss obstacles, ask questions when requirements are unclear, and remain open to feedback. A good team member should also be willing to adapt when requirements change and help identify ways to improve the development process. I believe that collaboration, accountability, and a willingness to learn are important qualities because successful software development depends on more than writing code. It also requires a team that can communicate effectively, solve problems together, and deliver software that meets user needs.
